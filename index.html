      3. Home Assistant System

      Local smart-home control plane: the Den hub, three wall Wallpanels, and one long-term bench set in the Den, with the §3.3 Device List. Radio hardware (ZBT-2 ×2 + ZWA-2) lands in §3.1. The IP side (Wi‑Fi / MQTT / Matter-over-Wi‑Fi) does not add another stick. Recording and access stay with UniFi Protect / Access. HA only automates across them and does not replace the NVR or the Access logic. Check HA Integrations and the community compatibility tables before buying a device. Prefer local control. Cloud is the fallback.

      Bluetooth / BLE: Everyday speech uses “Bluetooth” for two different things: Bluetooth Classic (audio, older serial, and the like) and Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) (low-power advertising and short connections). The Home Assistant Bluetooth integration supports BLE only, and does not support Classic. Short-range sensing in this house (BTHome temperature and humidity, and similar), some body-composition scales, and auxiliary presence detection use BLE. Walls attenuate it heavily and there is no mesh, so this does not rely on the Bluetooth radio in the hub chassis. It uses a room-level Bluetooth Proxy (remote adapter). As needed, and not on day 1: one or two Ethernet / PoE ESP32 boards per floor (an external antenna is preferred, such as the Olimex ESP32-PoE-ISO-EA), flashed with ESPHome Bluetooth Proxy, dedicated to BLE advertisements and short connections. The proxy board itself is wired, so it does not put Wi‑Fi and BLE on the same radio. The ZWA-2 is not the main BLE radio. Buy a vendor bridge (Lutron Caseta and the like) or an IR blaster for an infrared-only device only when one is actually needed. Important security does not depend on phone Bluetooth presence alone.

      Wi‑Fi: Devices that need high bandwidth, or that the vendor offers only over Wi‑Fi (some appliances, Matter-over-Wi‑Fi, a few IoT devices), use this house's UniFi APs. HA does not run a second Wi‑Fi network, and the hub does not get a USB Wi‑Fi stick. Throughput is high and onboarding is familiar, but the devices take AP airtime, a cloud-dependent model is crippled when the cloud drops, and 2.4 GHz contends with Zigbee / Thread. Brands with a clear local API are acceptable. A fixed device that can be wired (a room drop) is wired. This is a side channel, not the sensor backbone.

      MQTT: A software message bus, not a radio. It runs over Ethernet / Wi‑Fi / IP. This house installs Mosquitto on the hub when needed, for Tasmota / ESPHome, some DIY gear, and a later move to Zigbee2MQTT into HA. It decouples cleanly and can stay fully local. The broker and the discovery convention are run here. ZHA talking to Zigbee directly does not need MQTT for the sake of Zigbee. A large ESP fleet, or a move to Z2M, is when it becomes infrastructure.

      Matter: An application-layer standard. It can run on Thread or on Wi‑Fi / Ethernet. HA uses the Matter Server as the local controller. Matter-over-Thread goes through the second ZBT-2 Border Router in §3.1. Matter-over-Wi‑Fi goes over the LAN and does not use that stick. It is the long-term direction for local control across ecosystems. It is not a reason to tear down a Zigbee / Z-Wave network that is already stable. Thread / Matter are sensitive to IPv6 and mDNS. Leave the VLANs as they are until the firewall allows that traffic.

      3.1 Hub

      • ameriDroid PoE Mini PC for Home Assistant
        • Placement: in the Den, in the same room as the Mac Studio, on its own machine. It does not go in the equipment-room rack. Metal chassis, with VESA (under the desk or on the wall). The antenna bases of the three Connect sticks use the supplied cables and sit in an open part of the Den, about 0.5–1 m apart, away from USB 3, SSDs, display power supplies, and the nearest AP. Keep them out of a closed metal cavity. Where AP / Zigbee / Thread share 2.4 GHz, coexistence comes from separating the antenna bases and from channel planning.
        • OS and compute: Generic x86-64 Home Assistant OS (the vendor's BIOS preset includes power-on after power loss). The CPU is a quad-core Intel N100.
        • Memory and storage: settled at 16 GB DDR4 + 256 GB M.2 2280 SATA. 8 GB is tight for HAOS plus the protocol stacks, and 32 GB is unused headroom because this machine does not run Frigate or a large model. 128 GB fills up with backups and the recorder, and a larger drive or a 2.5″ drive is for media and long backups, which go to the NAS. On the order, name 16 GB / 256 GB under Custom build.
        • Network and power: PoE++ IN → the Den's second room drop → Pro XG 24 PoE 10G PoE+++. The Studio has the 10G drop to itself. One free 2.5G port, on a short patch, goes to the DATA IN of the PoE+ injector for the Den bench Wallpanel (see §3.2). The remaining 2.5G ports can stay free for later. Power is reserved at ≥30 W PoE++ (see PoE Power Breakdown). 12 V DC is only for setup and troubleshooting. Chassis USB: 1× USB-C + 1× USB 3.0-A + 4× USB 2.0-A. The three sticks each take a USB 2.0-A, where the radio noise is lower. USB-C and USB 3.0-A stay free.
        • Home Assistant Connect ZWA-2 (Z-Wave)
          • Wiring: the supplied USB-C to USB-A cable into one USB 2.0-A. Antenna placement follows the rule above.
          • Role: locks, some wall switches and outlets, and the control backbone that is more sensitive to penetration and interference. Sub‑GHz, off the 2.4 GHz Wi‑Fi band. It enters HA through Z-Wave JS: Z-Wave / Z-Wave Long Range (North America). It sits beside Zigbee as a local control backbone. The two stay in parallel. Locks prefer Z-Wave, or the UniFi Access already in the house, ahead of a Wi‑Fi cloud lock.
          • Tradeoff: wall penetration is usually better than a 2.4 GHz mesh. Long Range suits yard distances and does not take Wi‑Fi airtime. North American and European frequencies are not interchangeable, the ecosystem and the prices trail Zigbee, and the mesh still depends on mains-powered routing nodes. On a dropout, check that the antenna is in the open, that a routing node was not unplugged, that a wrong-region device was not added, and that USB 3 noise is not next to the controller. Certified locks and established wall switches (Zooz / Inovelli / Yale, and the like) are the steadier choice.
        • Home Assistant Connect ZBT-2 (Zigbee)
          • Wiring: the supplied USB-C to USB-A cable into one USB 2.0-A. Antenna placement follows the rule above.
          • Role: the largest local mesh, for sensors, bulbs, switches, and outlets. Fixed as Zigbee + ZHA (Thread stays on the other stick; the vendor does not recommend MultiPAN). A second Zigbee coordinator stays off the list. The hardware is close to the Thread stick. The networks do not talk to each other. This is the default first choice for a small number of devices that stay online. Where Zigbee is available, add fewer Wi‑Fi IoT devices.
          • Tradeoff: the devices are inexpensive, a mains-powered router brings the battery ends onto the mesh, and the ZHA path is short. It shares 2.4 GHz with Wi‑Fi / Thread, so the channels have to be planned. Some Tuya private clusters are incomplete on ZHA. Mesh quality follows the density of mains-powered nodes, rather than the coordinator's rated distance. The official lines from IKEA / Hue / Aqara (a bridge and a direct join behave differently) / Sonoff are steadier. For white-label battery sensors, lay the routing skeleton with outlets and lights first. Keep the antenna out of a closed metal cavity.
        • Home Assistant Connect ZBT-2 (Thread)
          • Wiring: the supplied USB-C to USB-A cable into one USB 2.0-A. Antenna placement follows the rule above.
          • Role: a low-power IPv6 mesh transport, mainly for Matter-over-Thread. This stick is fixed as the HA Border Router (Zigbee stays on the other stick, and it does not depend on a HomePod or a Nest). A third Thread stick waits unless a separate lab network comes later. For newly purchased battery Matter devices, this is the preferred transport. It leaves the Zigbee devices already on hand in place.
          • Tradeoff: it is built for battery devices, and through the border router they are reachable over IP. Thread itself is not an application layer. Bare Thread, with no Matter or vendor application, does nothing useful. Early devices are expensive, and it shares a band with Zigbee, so the antennas and the channels stay apart. Dropouts usually come from the border router losing power, from IPv6 / mDNS being broken by a VLAN, or from a single border router in a poor spot. An Apple TV or a Nest can be an extra border router later. This house treats the HA stick as the one that counts.

      3.2 Wallpanel

      Four identical core stacks for the house are in §3.3: three wall mounts (Basement / Main / Upper) and one long-term bench set in the Den. Each wall mount is budgeted at the 30 W IEEE 802.3at ceiling of the Waveshare PoE M.2 HAT+ (C) (see PoE Power Breakdown). The bench stand is settled as the Pimoroni Pibow Frame — Touch Display 2 10.1″ (purchase in §3.3). The wall-mount rear shell is in §3.2.1.

      • Wall mount ×3: Cat6A home run → patch panel → Pro XG 24 PoE 10G PoE+++ → HAT (C) RJ45.
      • Den bench ×1: a free 2.5G port on the §3.1 hub → short patch → PoE+ Adapter (30W · 1G) DATA IN. A wall AC outlet feeds the injector. Injector DATA+PoE OUT → HAT (C) RJ45. Power comes through the HAT, the same path as the wall units. The link negotiates 1G (the Pi 5's onboard gigabit).

      3.2.1 Wall-mount rear shell (3D print / sheet metal)

      The three wall mounts only. The STLs below are community starting points. None has been checked on this house's core stack (see §3.3). After they arrive they have to be test-fitted, and the depth or the openings may need a change. They can be combined: a full rear shell with VESA on the back, plus an off-the-shelf arm.

      • 3D print (ready-made models)
        • MakerWorld · Raspberry Pi 10.1″ Touch Display 2 Case (CreationThroughChaos) — made for the 10″ TD2. Today it is mainly a desk or rail case. The author says the wall-mount bracket is still in development. The Pi position and the display feet may need a small adjustment for tolerance.
        • Printables · Touch Display 2 wall mount case (fbm3334) — wall mount. The thick version is about 60 mm and leaves room for a HAT, with optional vent holes, and it includes the OpenSCAD source. The community mostly uses it for the 7″ TD2. Moving it to 10″ means re-parameterizing, or test-printing a revised model, to the 167×248 mm outline.
        • GitHub · cnxsoft VESA adapter (10″ TD2) — display-back M2.5 to 100×100 VESA. An off-the-shelf arm can go on the wall later. It does not enclose the Pi or the HAT. The open back leaves room for the Cooler and the HAT (C).
        • Printables · Frank Delporte wallmount (raised for PoE / HAT) — the network-port and vent openings are worth copying. The model is based on the 7″ TD2. 10″ needs a revised model to the outline.
        • Adafruit · Articulated Pi Display V2 Mount — the guide includes a Pi 5 + Active Cooler + M.2 HAT+ stack. The ST defaults to the 7″ TD2. Treat 10″ as a structural reference.
      • 3D print (modified here): take the stacked size of the real hardware, and deepen the cavity in OpenSCAD / CAD (Cooler exhaust + HAT (C) + 2230 + RJ45). PETG / ABS handles the temperature against a wall better than PLA. When printing the rear shell, keep the Active Cooler exhaust and the HAT (C) / RJ45 clearances.
      • Sheet metal: draw a STEP (outline matching the TD2, cavity clearing the Cooler + HAT (C) + 2230, openings for RJ45 / ventilation / optional VESA) → SendCutSend or a local laser and brake (aluminum 1.0–1.5 mm), then powder coat or anodize. It has to be drawn before it can be ordered.

      3.2.2 Software

      • System: Raspberry Pi OS (or a lighter image) boots from NVMe. A kiosk opens the HA dashboard full screen (Home Assistant Companion as the home app, or a browser shell such as Fully Kiosk). All four share one image baseline. The three wall mounts switch dashboard by floor. The Den bench can switch to any floor for a preview.
      • Accounts: each board has its own panel user. A wall mount's permissions cover only that floor's dashboard. The bench account can be wider, for debugging.
      • Screen on: a wall mount brightens when someone is near and dims when they leave (the onboard camera, or a presence sensor in the room). The bench can stay on, or use the same policy.
      • Build order: age the image on injector PoE in the Den and clone it, then cut openings for the three wall sets and put them on PoE+++. The bench set stays in the Den. Wiring is above.
